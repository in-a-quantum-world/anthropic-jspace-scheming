# Novelty and impact review (deep research memo, 2026-10-25)

> **Correction (2026-10-27, after external review).** An external reviewer found this memo's "narrowly and verifiably novel" confidence unsupported and several neighbour descriptions incomplete: PIR includes causal steering, cross-family evaluation and trained/external concealment organisms, so "recognition probe rather than any lens" undersells it; *Eliciting Secret Knowledge* (arXiv 2510.01070) is lens-involving recovery precedent this memo missed; the FIBS/*Caught in the Act* 0.63 is a hidden-goal-denial result, not a recovery ceiling, and v4 reports stronger numbers; SPAR Fall 2026 "The Signature of Scheming" overlaps directly. The memo's pilot numbers also predate the matched-distractor run, which failed two registered thresholds. The current positioning and numbers are in `docs/review-response.md`. The memo below was restyled for prose on 2026-10-28; its citations, numbers and conclusions are unchanged from the 2026-10-25 version, which remains in the git history.

The sprint project is sufficiently novel, impactful and alignment-relevant, subject to one condition. The exact combination this project is building, a Jacobian-lens readout that quantifies recovery of the specific answers a sandbagging model withholds, scored as rank-of-true-answer AUROC against matched distractors, with causal ablation, a comparison of prompted and password-locked-LoRA concealment, and a cross-model sweep, appears in no published paper, post, repository or dataset as of 25 October 2026. However, three projects in progress each hold a large fraction of it, and the available window is weeks rather than months. The nearest neighbours each stop short on an identifiable axis: PIR reads withheld knowledge from internals, explicitly for sandbagging audits, but uses a recognition probe rather than a lens ([arXiv 2609.21996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21996)); the Topic-Detector project quantifies the same core signal (true tokens elevated in J-space while the output withholds them) but studies censorship rather than sandbagging, uses one model, and its LoRA arm returned a negative result ([GitHub](https://github.com/Melchior-de-Polignac/Topic-Detector-Not-Lie-Detector)); Jimmy Liu's J-lens lie-detection post has the AUROC-with-logit-lens-baseline methodology but classifies lying rather than recovering answers, and currently sits rejected by LessWrong moderation ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/iSTJ6ZENhLDNJ7Yho/on-qwen-the-j-lens-readouts-at-the-newline-token-reveal-lies-3)). The case for impact is unusually concrete: UK AISI's White Box Control team names fine-tuned sandbagger detection and externally usable audit methods as open problems ([UK AISI update](https://alignmentforum.org/posts/pPEeMdgjpjHZWCDFw/white-box-control-at-uk-aisi-update-on-sandbagging)), and the strongest recent probe paper reports **63% AUC on its math-sandbagger, the weakest of its concealment models** ([Caught in the Act](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12445v1)). The literature nevertheless requires four design changes before the pilot numbers (93% top-20 persistence on gemma-4-E4B, 49% on 12B, distractor AUROC 0.914 on 12B) can be trusted: topic-matched distractors, text-independence controls, treating the prompted arm as confounded with the LoRA arm carrying the primary result, and re-running the main analyses with the J++ lens and a logit-lens baseline. The realistic venue is arXiv with an Alignment Forum linkpost now, upgraded to an ICLR 2027 workshop; NeurIPS 2026 workshop deadlines have almost certainly passed.

## The exact combination is unclaimed; six adjacent projects each stop short of it

A systematic sweep of arXiv, LessWrong/Alignment Forum, Hugging Face, the official repo, Neuronpedia, Apart Research sprints and BlueDot outputs found **no work that pairs a J-space readout with sandbagging and quantified true-answer recovery** ([novelty audit, KQ1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21996)). Searches for the combination came back empty across every channel checked: no sandbagging-related issue or PR on anthropics/jacobian-lens ([issues](https://github.com/anthropics/jacobian-lens/issues)), no Hugging Face artifact combining "jspace" with "sandbagging", and no behaviour research attached to Neuronpedia's hosting ([Neuronpedia blog](https://www.neuronpedia.org/blog/jacobian-lens)). More strikingly, no published work applies any lens, logit, tuned or Jacobian, to decode the answers a sandbagging model withholds on an evaluation: the sandbagging-internals literature uses probes and activation grafts that classify or unlock rather than read ([Auditing Games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07810); [Causal Model grafting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29461)), and lenses appear in the concealment literature only as baselines inside lie and censorship studies.

The adjacent projects define what this project can claim and what it cannot. The table shows each neighbour and the axis on which it stops short.

| Nearest neighbour | What it already holds | Where it falls short of the sprint design |

Two consequences follow. First, the novelty claim must be framed precisely: this would be the **first quantification of withheld-answer recovery under sandbagging through a workspace lens**, and not the first observation that true answers stay elevated under instructed concealment, because solarkyle's card already states that observation. Second, the prompted-versus-trained comparison is genuinely unsettled in the literature, which makes it the project's most valuable arm: Melchior's LoRA raised the raw conflict signal only **1.30×, mostly attributable to a global rescaling** ([Topic-Detector](https://github.com/Melchior-de-Polignac/Topic-Detector-Not-Lie-Detector)), and an Apart hackathon entry reported "Concealment Erases the Trace" ([Apart sprint](https://apartresearch.com/sprints/secret-loyalties-hackathon-2026-07-24-to-2026-07-26)), while PIR found **0.85–0.93 recognition on password-locked and circuit-broken checkpoints** ([PIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21996)). Either outcome of the LoRA arm would settle a live disagreement.

## Three competing efforts are in progress; the defence is speed and preregistration

The most likely near-term competing publications are the two pending posts. Liu's rejected post can resurface at any time, possibly improved with the steering and ablation experiments he explicitly lists as open questions ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/iSTJ6ZENhLDNJ7Yho/on-qwen-the-j-lens-readouts-at-the-newline-token-reveal-lies-3)); Melchior's companion LessWrong post is written and "will be added on publication", and when it appears it establishes priority on quantified J-space true-token elevation under concealment with a distractor-style control and a trained-in arm ([GitHub](https://github.com/Melchior-de-Polignac/Topic-Detector-Not-Lie-Detector)). Neither covers sandbagging, per-answer rank AUROC, capability locks or scale, so neither would end the project, but each would reduce what it can claim first. The third risk is PIR's author iterating: v2 arrived five days after v1, and a v3 that swaps the recognition probe for a lens readout or adds AUROC would be a direct collision; the sensible response is to use PIR as a baseline and comparison point rather than to ignore it.

One structural risk deserves naming: **Kola Ayonrinde is an author on both the Auditing Games for Sandbagging paper and the J++ Lens**, which means the UK AISI/Anthropic Fellows cluster contains at least one person holding both halves of this project ([Auditing Games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07810); [J++ post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/nc9dHfcB22JdMzGbr/j-lens-jacobian-filtering-enables-more-faithful-workspace-lenses)). The unknowns compound this: the SPAR Fall-2026 project list was surfaced but never read ([SPAR F26](https://sparai.org/projects/f26/)), 177 Apart submissions were only partially examined, the official repo's 292 forks are unaudited, and Caught in the Act's Appendix F.1 "activation oracles" cross-check on the math-sandbagger went unread and could sit closer to answer readout than its summary suggests ([Caught in the Act](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12445v1)).

Three measures address this. First, timestamp now: the ecosystem norm is preregistration (Melchior committed his design before results, and solarkyle SHA-256-hashed frozen classifiers before Stage-2 data), so a design document with hashes committed to the repo (github.com/in-a-quantum-world/anthropic-jspace-scheming) this week establishes priority cheaply. Second, publish interim lab notes early rather than waiting for the full result: UK AISI itself publishes "preliminary lab-notes-style updates" on the Alignment Forum from samples as small as 17 transcripts, and the genre is accepted when caveated ([UK AISI update](https://alignmentforum.org/posts/pPEeMdgjpjHZWCDFw/white-box-control-at-uk-aisi-update-on-sandbagging)). An Alignment Forum note at the end of week two establishes the framing publicly even if a neighbour publishes first. Third, differentiate by combining the axes no single neighbour holds: the sandbagging evaluation framing, per-answer distractor-rank AUROC, concealment-concept ablation, a capability-lock LoRA, and multiple models. Caught in the Act's appendix and the SPAR F26 list should both be read before the write-up asserts priority.

## The literature requires design changes before the pilot numbers can be trusted

Four published results bear directly on whether the pilot data means what it appears to mean, and each demands a concrete change.

**Topic-matched distractors are required.** Melchior's single most important finding is that benign same-topic prompts produce an association fraction of **0.894**: "most cross-topic salience is topic association, not a per-utterance lie signal" ([Topic-Detector](https://github.com/Melchior-de-Polignac/Topic-Detector-Not-Lie-Detector)). If the pilot's distractors differ in topic from the true answers, the **0.914 AUROC** may be measuring topic salience rather than withheld-answer persistence. Distractors must match the true answer on topic, answer type and plausibility, and ideally on token frequency. The multiple-choice setting helps here: when all options sit in the prompt, ranking the true option above equally present distractor options is itself a partial control, but a free-form arm with the answer string absent from context is the clean version. Tokenisation adds a constraint the J++ authors flag: multi-digit numbers are invisible in Qwen readouts and the lens only reads single-vocabulary-token concepts ([J++ post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/nc9dHfcB22JdMzGbr/j-lens-jacobian-filtering-enables-more-faithful-workspace-lenses)), so the item pool should be filtered to answers that are single tokens in every model studied, or scored on first tokens with that choice reported.

**Prompted-only results are now discounted, so the LoRA arm carries the primary result.** The field converged on this through three routes: Boxo et al. showed sandbagging probe AUROC falls **0.94 → 0.57** once textual evidence of the behaviour is removed ([arXiv 2509.21344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21344)); UK AISI's stated worry is that probes detect the instruction rather than the cognition ([UK AISI update](https://alignmentforum.org/posts/pPEeMdgjpjHZWCDFw/white-box-control-at-uk-aisi-update-on-sandbagging)); and the workspace paper itself documents that modulation is phrasing-sensitive, with "ignore X" still activating X ([Gurnee et al.](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html)). In the prompted arm the sandbagging instruction sits in context and may itself push the withheld answer into the workspace, so prompted persistence is confounded by construction. The password-locked LoRA, tested with the password absent, carries no instruction text at all, and any true-answer elevation there is genuinely internal. Varying instruction phrasings in the prompted arm (including some that never mention answering wrongly) gives a dose-response check on the confound. Off-the-shelf password-locked and circuit-broken models from the van der Weij line are public on Hugging Face (FelixHofstaetter gemma/mistral variants), which buys comparability and saves compute alongside a self-trained LoRA ([van der Weij et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07358)).

**The 93% vs 49% scale gap is confounded three ways and needs normalisation.** First, lens quality varies sharply across models: the J-lens achieves only 35.7% recall@10 on Qwen3.6-27B where **J++ reaches 55.2%**, with a median **63.5% relative uplift** across six models ([J++ post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/nc9dHfcB22JdMzGbr/j-lens-jacobian-filtering-enables-more-faithful-workspace-lenses)), so lower persistence on a larger model may only mean a worse lens fit. Second, read depth shifts with scale: solarkyle found the informative signal at 31B lives in the deepest quarter of the fitted band while a fixed mid-band average that works at 4B–12B goes flat ([solarkyle/jspace-lenses](https://huggingface.co/solarkyle/jspace-lenses)). Third, 4B gemma versus 12B gemma versus 27B Qwen crosses families and tokenisers, so "scale" is entangled with family. The fixes: normalise each model's sandbagged persistence by its honest-condition persistence ceiling (same items, no lock), sweep read depth per model, and re-run the main numbers with J++ where Neuronpedia hosts it. Neuronpedia's J++ fits include an in-family Qwen ladder (Qwen3.5-0.8B at 44.9%, 2B at 51.5%, Qwen3-4B at 53.8%, Qwen3.6-27B at 55.2% recall@10) ([Neuronpedia card](https://huggingface.co/neuronpedia/jacobian-lens)), which is the clean instrument for a real scale claim at near-zero compute, with the gemma pair demoted to a family-robustness check. Gupta's corpus-dependence result adds a reporting obligation: changing only the fitting corpus moves readout scores materially and between-source variation exceeds sampling variation, so the fitting corpus of every lens used is part of the measurement specification ([NeurIPS poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/165983)). Since this project's trio matches solarkyle's registry exactly, that means documenting his fitting recipe and, for one model, checking sensitivity against a Neuronpedia or self-fitted lens. A practical note: solarkyle verified that a bf16-fitted lens reads NF4 4-bit activations without refitting at 12B ([solarkyle/jspace-lenses](https://huggingface.co/solarkyle/jspace-lenses)), which makes the 12B and 27B runs feasible on Colab Pro hardware.

**The causal ablation needs the controls that ended the eval-awareness line.** Two independent 2026 results showed that steering the eval-awareness direction neither uncovers nor induces sandbagging: effects were bounded at **≤5.7pp** and reproduced by random directions ([Huang & Han](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/loc/sydney/178340)), and a preregistered steering attempt swung behaviour by only −1.77 points, failing its own 5-point bar and not beating 24 random directions ([sahilraut](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ogvyWqJtSrpgXfc7t/eval-awareness-steering-detects-the-test-not-the-sabotage)). Any ablation of concealment concepts therefore needs norm-matched random-direction controls, a benign-item accuracy check (steering strong enough to change behaviour often degrades everything equally), and a preregistered effect-size bar. If ablating concealment vectors recovers answers where eval-awareness steering is known to fail, that contrast is itself a leading result. Two further complications: per-item ground truth for "which items were sandbagged" is unstable, with two labelling oracles agreeing on only **~54% of items** ([sahilraut](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ogvyWqJtSrpgXfc7t/eval-awareness-steering-detects-the-test-not-the-sabotage)), so sandbagged items should be defined by a multi-sample honest-versus-locked accuracy gap with agreement against a noise-injection oracle reported ([Tice et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01784)); and optimising against the lens invites Goodhart effects, as Ziruibest's unlearning penalty showed when revival *increased* under a J-occupancy loss ([Ziruibest](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Ziruibest/jspace-unlearning)), so the write-up should explicitly scope the method to audit time and never to use as a training signal.

Finally, baselines decide whether the J-lens itself survives the paper. Liu found the logit lens within 0.01 AUROC of the J-lens on lie detection, while Melchior found a 0.966-versus-0.725 gap on censored-topic conflict. The J-lens's marginal value for sandbagging is an open empirical question, and answering it requires running the logit lens on every analysis, J++ where available, and a prompted black-box monitor as the field-standard comparison ([trusted monitoring precedent](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/eaEqAzGN3uJfpfGoc/trusted-monitoring-but-with-deception-probes)).

## Position it as elicitation for external auditors, not a deployed monitor

The demand side is unusually concrete for a sprint-scale project. UK AISI's White Box Control team states its current focus as "detecting sandbagging, where models strategically underperform during evaluations" ([UK AISI](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/why-were-working-on-white-box-control)), and its published open-problems list aligns closely with this project's specification: why fine-tuned sandbaggers are much harder for probes than prompted ones, and "producing evidence that decision-makers will find legible" ([UK AISI update](https://alignmentforum.org/posts/pPEeMdgjpjHZWCDFw/white-box-control-at-uk-aisi-update-on-sandbagging)). The December 2025 auditing game concluded that no single reliable method exists and that the one dependable mitigation, on-distribution training, "requires training access that external evaluators often lack" ([UK AISI blog](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/auditing-games-for-sandbagging-detection)); a lens readout needs only weights and a forward pass, which places it squarely in that acknowledged gap. Anthropic's recommended directions ask for exactly this reliability work under "activation monitoring" ([Anthropic](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/recommended-directions/)), and the quantified ceiling for pure-internals recovery of prompted sandbagging currently sits at roughly **26% via SAE steering against 83% for a prompt suffix** ([SAE steering post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/dBckLjYfTShBGZ8ma/can-sae-steering-reveal-sandbagging)), so internals-based recovery is the weakest known lever and an obvious target.

Framing matters more than usual here. The auditing game's fragility critiques attach to classifiers deployed as monitors, which red teams defeat with distractor behaviours; an information-presence claim ("the withheld answer is readable in the workspace") belongs to the tuned-lens genre of elicitation rather than monitoring, and avoids that line of criticism ([impact analysis of 2512.07810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07810)). The OpenAI/Hugging Face sandbox-escape incident strengthens the premise without being overclaimed: every published remediation, including OpenAI's own report, is behavioural (CoT monitoring, tripwires, egress control), and no source found calls for internals-based checks during evaluations ([OpenAI incident report](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/67869394-cb91-4c12-888c-5cbd85c7814c/OpenAI-Hugging-Face%20Incident-Technical-Report.pdf)), so "the missing internals layer in eval-time monitoring" is unclaimed positioning, with the honest caveat that the incident itself was specification gaming rather than sandbagging. The write-up should also carry one responsible-use paragraph: the ecosystem has already produced J-space-guided abliteration for uncensoring models ([jwest33](https://huggingface.co/jwest33/qwen3.5-9b-jspace-abliterated)), and a recovery method is also an extraction method, so scoping to audit contexts is both honest and protective. Zvi's warning that training against workspace readouts pushes cognition "into the shadow" belongs in the limitations ([Zvi](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/no-space-like-j-space)).

Reception follows a known checklist distilled from what succeeded in this genre: AUROC plus TPR at 1–2% FPR calibrated on a benign control set, a prompted black-box monitor baseline, at least one fine-tuned concealment model, an explicit confound control in the spirit of the ICLR 2026 format-sensitivity critique ([ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10014626)), honest negative results, and released code. On venue: NeurIPS 2026 workshops run 11–13 December but their submission deadlines historically fall in late August to early September and none could be verified as still open ([NeurIPS blog](https://blog.neurips.cc/?p=1686)), so the December-timescale path is arXiv plus an Alignment Forum linkpost, matching the stated arXiv plan, then an upgrade to ICLR 2027 workshops (deadlines around February) or the ICML 2027 MechInterp workshop. The best-fit audiences are "Interpretability as a Science", "Trustworthy AI Evaluation" and "Can We Trust the Judge?", the latter two dovetailing with the existing evaluation-validity research thread. A ~300-word expression of interest to Coefficient Giving's rolling TAIS RFP costs almost nothing and fits the interpretability/control/evals areas ([Coefficient listing](https://grantedai.com/grants/coefficient-giving-rfp-technical-ai-safety-research-alignment-interpretability-ai-control)); the BlueDot staged grant's second tranche is the funding line for the LoRA arm.

## Conclusion

The conclusion is to continue, with the LoRA arm promoted from a robustness check to the primary experiment. The literature's trajectory through 2026 systematically devalued what the pilot's prompted arm shows (probes and readouts keep turning out to detect the instruction, the framing or the topic rather than the cognition) while leaving exactly one version of the claim both unclaimed and decision-relevant: that the specific answers a model was *trained* to withhold remain readable in its workspace, quantified per answer against matched distractors, by a method an external auditor can run without training access. If that number is high, it is the first demonstration that lens-based elicitation beats the known 26% internals ceiling on the hardest concealment type probes face; if it is low, it extends Melchior's negative result into the sandbagging domain and sharpens the case that trained concealment erases the workspace trace, which the field would equally cite. The pilot's most intriguing datum, the 93%-to-49% persistence drop with scale, is currently uninterpretable until lens quality, read depth and family are controlled, but if it survives normalisation it becomes the project's most consequential finding: workspace readability of concealed knowledge declining with scale would matter to every white-box audit agenda at once, and the prefitted Qwen J++ ladder means testing it costs almost nothing.

The deeper shift this research forced is that the project's risk is no longer whether it is novel (it is, narrowly and verifiably) but whether the claim will survive the five controls that the last four months of literature made mandatory. Every one of those controls is implementable within the sprint on the existing hardware and lens registry. Speed is the binding constraint and rigour the differentiator, and no neighbouring project has shipped the combination.
