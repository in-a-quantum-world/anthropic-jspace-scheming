## Core method

- [ ] Anthropic, *Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace in Language Models* - https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html - the J-space paper. Author list inconsistent across community citations (Gurnee et al. vs Lindsey et al.): settle it from the page.
- [ ] anthropics/jacobian-lens (GitHub) - reference implementation, Apache 2.0, includes the fitting script. The `jlens` library the pilot uses.
- [ ] neuronpedia/jacobian-lens (HF collection) - Neuronpedia's lens fits, includes a Pythia-70m lens used by others as a validation target.
- [ ] Lens lineage, for the paper's related work: logit lens (nostalgebraist, LessWrong 2020), tuned lens (Belrose et al., arXiv 2303.08112, verify).
- [x] **koayon/jpp-lenses** - https://hf.co/koayon/jpp-lenses - J++ Lens fits (Ayonrinde & Lindsey, LessWrong 2026: "J++ Lens: Jacobian Filtering Enables More Faithful Workspace Lenses"). Covers **Qwen/Qwen3.6-27B** (our checkpoint; recall@10 55.2%, layers 0-62, 6.6 GB fp32), plus Qwen3.5-9B, gemma-4-31B, Olmo-3-32B, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, DeepSeek-V4-Flash. File format documented (torch dict, `parameters["jacobians"]`); readout = unembed(J_l h). Code: github.com/safety-research/jpp_lens (current address; the older koayon/jpp_lens does not resolve). Verified from the repo README 2026-10-25; used as our lens-quality control.

## Review-driven additions (2026-10-27, from the external design review)

- [x] **PIR / *A Lie Detector Test for Language Models: Reading Knowledge a Model Won't Reveal*** - arXiv 2609.21996 - title verified by search 2026-10-27. Per the review (of the 2026-09-23 version): causal steering tests, evaluation across model families, AUC-based concealment detection, trained and external concealment organisms, free-form experiments with reconstructed answer candidates (those details: verify against the paper). The closest method neighbour. The distinction is candidate-conditioned recognition versus a direct pre-answer vocabulary readout, and any advantage we claim has to be practical: fewer forward passes, no candidate presentation, or recovery when output sampling misses.
- [x] ***Eliciting Secret Knowledge from Language Models*** - arXiv 2510.01070 (also OpenReview 7SnghonEMY); Cywinski, Ryd, Wang, Rajamanoharan, Nanda, Conmy, Marks (MATS) - read 2026-10-30 (v2 abstract page). Three families of secret-keeping organisms (e.g. a model that applies the user's gender downstream while denying knowing it); an LLM auditor guesses the secret, and each elicitation technique is scored by whether it helps the auditor. Prefill attacks are the strongest technique in every setting; logit-lens and SAE readouts raise auditor success but trail prefill. Organisms and code released as a public elicitation benchmark. Consequences for us: the P0 budgeted black-box baseline should be a prefill attack, the strongest black-box elicitor on trained concealers; the auditor-success protocol is a scoring template for candidate recovery; and they apply white-box tools to the trained organism without asking whether the training degraded the tools, which is the gap the parent-vs-refitted matrix fills. Precedent for lens-based recovery of concealed information, so prior lenses cannot be described as classification-only.
- [x] ***Telling FIBS: Probes Effectively Detect Sabotage and Catch Unverbalized Deception*** - arXiv 2610.12445, formerly *Caught in the Act*; retitle verified by search 2026-10-27. Review corrections: the 0.63 concerns hidden-goal denials, not withheld-answer recovery; v4 (2026-10-20) reportedly adds an activation-oracle 0.80 and a probe rise 0.63 to 0.93 with a relevant preceding interaction (verify against v4). Not a ceiling our answer-recovery AUROC can be compared against.
- [x] **SPAR Fall 2026, "The Signature of Scheming: Cross-Organism Interpretability of Strategic Misrepresentation"** - sparai.org/projects/f26/rec8h4EE5tioWPoGA - page read 2026-10-30. Mentors David Williams-King (ERA), Linh Le (Lida Safety), Hong Kiat Tan (UCLA); 3-5 mentees at 10 h/week, running this autumn. Builds organisms for sandbagging, secret-keeping, alignment faking and secret loyalties via SFT, RL and possibly emergent misalignment, then looks for a shared concealment signature across them; tests activation oracles, natural-language autoencoders and J-space against probe and SAE baselines. Cites a prior finding that sandbagging directions do not transfer between training mechanisms (SFT to RL). Consequences for us: occupied adjacent space and a collaboration lead; our distinction is instrument validity (parent vs refitted) and answer recovery rather than a shared signature; the no-transfer finding becomes a stated limitation of any single-training-mechanism matrix; their application questions cite the auditing-games paper and J-space sandbagging detection, confirming the framing is current.
- [ ] ***J-space auditing might be unreliable*** - lesswrong.com/posts/69fachkoAs2ZHeSst/j-space-auditing-might-be-unreliable-1 (a sibling post exists at slug 7Qi3LR9ZXpvptLp6m) - existence verified by search 2026-10-27. Checkpoint and lens distribution-shift concerns; background for our parent-versus-refitted lens comparison.
- [ ] **Pranay Narula, public J-space readouts under instructed lying** (LinkedIn) - the review verified the public description only, with no quantitative report; the write-up should be requested before this is cited as evidence.
- Code address correction: the J++ implementation lives at **github.com/safety-research/jpp_lens** (the paper's own link); the older koayon/jpp_lens address does not resolve.
- [x] **Auditing Games for Sandbagging** - arXiv 2512.07810 (Taylor, Black, Read, Golechha, Zelenka-Martin, Makins, Kissane, Ayonrinde, Merizian, Bloom, UK AISI; Bowen, Cundy, FAR.AI; Marks, Anthropic) - read 2026-10-30 (HTML v1). Blinded red/blue auditing game: five models, two password-locked sandbaggers (Cedar: broad underperformance by logit-matching a weaker model, special-token password; Oak: narrow, Intercode CTF only) and three benign models with distractor quirks. Blue team had raw weights; phase 1 (no training) classified 1 of 5: transcript analysis was defeated by weak-model imitation, and a deception probe that did flag Cedar was distrusted for lack of validation amid distractor false positives. Phase 2: single-sample on-distribution fine-tuning fully elicited the sandbaggers (2 of 5 classified; Oak missed for time). Recommendation: elicitation by training over detection in the short term. Lock gates used: at least a 10-point password gap at McNemar p < 0.05, benign controls lose at most 5 points. Consequences for us: the probe-distrust failure is the calibration gap the matrix addresses (a correct instrument nobody could validate); their lock gates join the MATS checklist in prereg Amendment 2; their own stated limitation, that password-locked models may be easier to elicit than real sandbaggers, applies to our organism and goes in our limitations verbatim.\n- Further items surfaced while verifying the above (all unread, (verify)): *Liars' Bench* (arXiv 2511.16035, NeurIPS 2026); *Same Question, Different Lies: C3 for Black-Box Sandbagging Detection* (PMLR v306); *Option-Order Randomisation Reveals a Distributional Position Attractor in Prompted Sandbagging* (arXiv 2604.26206).
